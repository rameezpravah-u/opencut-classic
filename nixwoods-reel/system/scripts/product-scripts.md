# Scripts — the four top products (6 Sep 2026)

Written against the 90-day numbers, not taste. Each product gets five reels: a **stopper** (top of funnel, highest CTR pattern), a **saver** (reference content people keep), a **sharer** (identity or transformation), a **convincer** (mid funnel, mechanism + proof) and a **closer** (bottom funnel, price and terms).

Ranked by 90-day net sales: Aurora ₹525k / 194 orders · Rubik's ₹347k / 121 · Rosewood Pendant ₹124k / 13 · Double Arm Teak ₹59k / 4.

**The CTR/ROAS split that drives the writing.** Wall lights carry the account's highest CTR (5.56% to women 35–64) and Aurora's workhorse runs 3.66% at 3.64x. The pendants are the mirror image: the Rosewood static drew 1.52% CTR but returned **13.1x**, and the Nordic pendant 14.9x. So wall-light copy is written to *stop* — short contrast claims, a visible before and after. Pendant copy is written to *qualify* — it should be ignorable by everyone who does not own a dining table, and unmissable by the person who does. Price early on pendants, late on the lamp.

Caption rules: ≤12 words a screen, on by 0.3 s, held ≥1.2 s, never over the fixture. Voice-over 130–150 wpm.

## Save and share triggers used throughout

- **Saves** come from reference value. Every saver reel is a sizing or spec card people will come back to, and ends on a recap frame rather than a sell.
- **Shares** come from identity — the sharer reels are written so sending one says something about the sender ("this is my taste", "this is the fix for your ugly ceiling light").
- **Loops.** The colour-loop and kinetic cuts end near their opening frame so a replay is seamless and watch time counts twice.
- **Comment bait, honest only.** A real either/or on the saver reels ("36 or 48 for a 5-foot console?") earns replies, which rank above likes. Never a fake error.
- **Muted first.** 85% watch without sound; the caption carries the whole argument, and the voice-over is a bonus, never the load-bearing part.
