# Teak linear-light assembly reel — build notes

**Deliverable:** `reel.mp4` — 9:16, 1080×1920, 25 fps, 26.56 s, music mix.
**Cost:** zero. No generation API was called. Every frame comes from assets already in
`nixwoods-reel/`.

---

## 1. What the competitor reel is, and what we took from it

Reference: `instagram.com/reel/DbVnOOksf-c` — `alumaetg`, an Iranian aluminium
lighting-profile maker. 28 s, 3D assembly animation: black aluminium extrusion on a bench →
LED strip lowers into the channel → diffuser slides in → end cap → ceiling bracket and
suspension → finished pendant lit over a desk → logo. Caption is a direct-from-manufacturer
pitch (every stage in-house, no middlemen, better price).

This was not new ground: `nixwoods-reel/linear-reels/README.md` already documents that reel,
and `system/briefs/L2-linear-teak.json` is a brief written *specifically* to answer it in teak.
So the job was to **render the brief that already existed**, not to invent a treatment. I changed
exactly one creative value — the grade on the workshop beat, which rendered illegible; see §4.
Everything else (shot list, durations, camera, text, music, end card) is the brief as written.

**None of their footage is used.** What was taken is the shot list and the cut timings, which
are not theirs to own. Their cut points were measured off the source at 10 fps and recorded in
the brief as: **8.1 / 0.4 / 3.1 / 1.5 / 11.9 / 0.6 / 2.9 s** (seven takes, 28.5 s). Our shot
durations reproduce those exactly — see the beat table.

The one deliberate divergence: they end on the product, we end on the product *after* a beat of
real workshop footage. Their "every stage in-house" claim had to be sold in CGI. Ours is
literally true, and there is real footage of it, so the proof goes last.

## 3. Beats we had no asset for, and how they were derived

Nine of the thirteen beats are covered by **real NixWoods teak photography or real workshop
footage**. Four are not — the assembly stages, because no NixWoods linear bar has ever been
photographed on a bench. Here is the full provenance chain, because it matters for disclosure:

1. **Stages 1 and 2 were AI-generated earlier** (ElevenLabs `bytedance-seedream-5-pro`,
   818 credits each) as **rosewood** — `gen-stage1-channel.png`, `gen-stage2-led.png`.
2. **Stages 3 and 4 were never generated** — the image quota ran out (139 credits left of a
   needed 818). They were **derived from stage 2 in numpy/ffmpeg at zero cost**:
   - *diffuser*: the bright LED dots blurred along the channel into a continuous line, with the
     near end left un-blurred, so a wipe across it reads as a diffuser sliding in;
   - *lit*: a warm bloom screen-composited off the brightest pixels.
3. **The teak recolour**: all four stages were recoloured from rosewood to teak, with the
   target sampled from real NixWoods teak photography. This is the `t_*` set the brief uses.

**The "part slides into place" motion is not generated video.** It is a `wipe` shot type: a
left-to-right reveal between two consecutive still stages. Three of them cover beats 2, 3 and 4.
That is how a component appears to move without paying for a video model.

**The end cap beat (5) has no asset at all.** It is covered by a 0.4 s hard punch-in on the end
of a real teak bar (`sh01-underside-twin.jpg` at `crop_cx 0.32`) — a flash cut, which is what
their 0.4 s take is too. The literal end-cap photo we own (`sh04-end-channels.webp`) was
rejected on resolution — see §4.

## 4. What was measured and checked

**Source resolution vs. the frame** (the brief claims these; I re-measured and they hold):

| Asset | Native | Fit | Upscale to 1080×1920 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sh01-underside-twin.jpg` | 1254² | cover | **1.53×** | used |
| `sh03-dark-two-lines.jpg` | 1170² | cover | **1.64×** | used |
| `sh05-canopy-cables.jpg` | 819² | contain | **1.29×** | used |
| `sh00-marble-dining.jpg` | 1254² | contain | 0.84× (downscale) | used |
| `sh04-end-channels.webp` | **493²** | contain | **2.15×** | **rejected — visibly soft** |
| `sh02-strip-fluted.jpg` | **595²** | contain | **1.78×** | **rejected — visibly soft** |
| teak stages | 2048×1152 | — | downscale | fine |

The two rejected photos were the original choices for beats 5 and 11; both were swapped for
crops of `sh01`/`sh03`, which need no meaningful upscale. Structure and durations unchanged.

**Teak colour, measured** (mean of warm mid-luma pixels, i.e. the wood):

| | hue | sat |
|---|---|---|
| original generated rosewood (`gen-stage1`) | 18.7° | 0.15 |
| teak stages 1–4 (`t_*`) | 23–26° | 0.42–0.44 |
| **real NixWoods teak** (`sh01-underside-twin`) | **29.9°** | **0.41** |

The recolour lands the saturation on the real teak almost exactly (0.42 vs 0.41) and moves hue
+5–8° from rosewood toward teak, but still sits **~4–6° redder than the real product**. Under
the noir grade and 0.35 s dissolves this is not obvious, but it is a real gap and worth closing
if this ever runs as a paid ad next to a product photo.

**Derivation actually landed** (channel crop, stages 2→3→4): stage 3 shows the LED chips
dissolved into a continuous line in the far half while the near end still shows discrete chips
(the intended half-in diffuser); stage 4 is **+13 mean luma** (168.8 vs 155.5) with the hot-pixel
fraction up from 29% to 34%. The difference is invisible in a thumbnail but correct at 1:1,
which is how it is shown.

**Source-overrun check**: beat 10 asks for `ss 2.1 + 3.7 s = 5.80 s` of `IMG_7745.mp4`, which is
**5.93 s** long. It fits, with 0.13 s to spare. (This guard matters: an earlier version of this
brief asked 3.9 s of a 3.1 s clip and would have silently truncated everything after it.)

**The music bed is shorter than the reel.** `music7-noir.mp3` is **20.04 s**; the reel runs
26.56 s. `amix=duration=longest` therefore ended the mix at 20 s, leaving the dining-room payoff
and the whole end card **silent** — and because the mux used `-shortest`, one build came out
truncated to exactly 20.00 s. The bed is now looped (`-stream_loop -1`) and trimmed to length.
Verified after the fix: mean volume **−13.3 dB** over 0–5 s and **−14.2 dB** over 21–26.5 s, so
audio actually runs to the end. Worth picking a longer track for this cut — the loop point at
20 s is audible if you listen for it.

**The payoff beat was graded into mud — found and fixed.** Beat 10 is the workshop footage
carrying "we make it ourselves.", 3.7 s, the longest single beat in the reel. The brief stacks
`eq=brightness=-0.12:contrast=1.06` on top of a noir grade that already carries
`brightness=-0.04`, `contrast=1.16` and a curve pulling 0.3→0.29. The raw frame is fine
(mean luma **121**, p10 38); the graded result was **mean 40.7 with p10 clipped to 0** — a
blue-black smear with the key line sitting on top of nothing. Changed to
`eq=brightness=0.07:contrast=1.03:saturation=1.06`, giving **mean 90.6, p10 26**: the bench,
the boards and the workshop behind them are all readable, and the line has something to land on.
This is the only creative change I made, and it lives in **`L2b-linear-teak.json`** (a copy of
the brief with that one field changed — the original `L2-linear-teak.json` in the repo is
untouched). `reel.mp4` is built from L2b.

**A beat that was being thrown away — found and fixed.** The brief puts a 0.35 s dissolve on
every boundary, including both sides of the 0.4 s end-cap flash cut (beat 5). 0.35 + 0.35 s of
dissolve does not fit in a 0.40 s shot. The monolithic renderer does not complain — it simply
lets the outgoing and incoming dissolves **overlap**, so that shot never reaches full opacity
and the end-cap beat is effectively a smear between its neighbours. Beat 11 (0.6 s) had the
same problem. Rendering shot-by-shot surfaced it as a hard error:

```
shot 4 is shorter than its two dissolves (10f vs 9+9f)
```

The renderer now shrinks the two dissolves around a short shot until at least one frame of it
plays clean — beat 5 to 4f in / 5f out, beat 11 to 7f / 7f. Both flash cuts now actually read,
which is what their 0.4 s and 0.6 s takes are. Side effect: runtime goes from 26.25 s to
**26.56 s** (+14 frames), slightly closer to their 28 s.

**Preflight** (from the system's own gate): ✓ brand rules, ✓ ad rules, ✓ facts, ✓ hook on screen
by 0.3 s and holds 1.2 s, ✓ all text in the safe zone, ✓ length. Open items are the ones only a
human can close: landing page, a named control in the same ad set, a kill threshold, and the
**AI disclosure tick in Ads Manager**.

## 5. Things you should know before posting

- **Runtime is 26.56 s, not 28.5 s.** The twelve 0.35 s dissolves overlap, so 28.5 s of shot
  plus a 2.0 s end card lands at 26.56 s. If matching their 28 s matters, set
  `"transition": "cut"` in the brief and it comes back to 28.5 s. I left the dissolves in —
  they are what makes the assembly read as one continuous take.
- **The four assembly stages are AI-origin.** Real teak photography covers nine of thirteen
  beats, but the bench assembly does not exist as a photograph. **Any paid placement needs
  Meta's AI disclosure ticked.** Organic is fine as is.
- **Hook gate is 8/12, pull ✗.** "this one is not aluminium." opens a gap (what *is* it?) that
  the assembly answers, which is why it beat "everyone makes these in aluminium." (4/12). It
  still has no pull device — no question, no count-in. Worth an A/B if this performs.
- Nothing was committed to git, per the brief of this task.

## 6. How it was actually rendered (and why there is a script here)

`python3 system/make_reel.py system/briefs/L2-linear-teak.json --audio music` **fails in this
container.** It builds one ffmpeg filtergraph in which all 13 shots are decoded at 2×
supersample (2160×3840, see `reelkit.still_chain`) and held alive simultaneously while the xfade
chain folds them together. The kernel killed it:

```
Memory cgroup out of memory: Killed process 1874 (ffmpeg)
total-vm:11472424kB, anon-rss:4987512kB
```

This is a container limit, not a bug in the reel system: `linear-reels/out/system/` holds a full
set of L2 intermediates from 17 Sep whose `-clean.log` ends in a complete x264 summary
(`frame=656 ... Lsize=48779KiB`), so the same brief rendered fine on a bigger box. The mp4s were
simply not kept — the repo has a size ceiling on committed renders. Another session was also
running a monolithic render in this container while I worked (two ffmpegs at 4.6 GB and 3.1 GB
RSS), which is very likely what tipped my first attempt over the cgroup limit. The staged
renderer peaks at ~0.6 GB and is unaffected.

`render_lowmem.py` (in this folder) fixes the *execution*, not the creative. It still calls
`mechanisms.build()`, so shot list, durations, camera moves, grade, text layers, end card and
audio all come from the brief and `presets.json` exactly as before. Only the ffmpeg strategy
changes:

| stage | what | peak cost |
|---|---|---|
| A | one ffmpeg per shot → `segNN.mp4` | one supersampled chain, not thirteen |
| B | one ffmpeg per dissolve → `transNN.mp4` | two 1080×1920 inputs, 0.35 s each |
| C | the non-overlapping body of each shot → `bodyNN.mp4` | trivial |
| D | concat demuxer, `-c copy` → `base.mp4` | no re-encode |
| E1 | burn the four text cue PNGs → `vonly.mp4` | one video input |
| E2 | music + 2 whooshes, mixed and loudness-normalised → `aonly.m4a` | audio only |
| E3 | mux, `-c copy`, `+faststart` → `reel.mp4` | trivial |

Two ffmpeg traps showed up on the way and are fixed in the script, with the diagnosis in
comments so the next person does not re-find them:

1. **`trim` erases the frame rate**, and `xfade` then refuses the stream
   (`The inputs needs to be a constant frame rate; current rate of 1/0 is invalid`).
   Both branches re-assert `fps`/`settb` before the xfade.
2. **A `-c copy` concat of differently-tagged pieces deadlocks the next pass.** The stills
   carried no colour metadata, the workshop clip was tagged bt709, so `base.mp4` changed
   parameters mid-stream. ffmpeg logged *"Reconfiguring filter graph because video parameters
   changed to yuv420p(tv, bt709)"* and then hung: 55 threads in `futex_do_wait`, CPU time
   frozen at 96 s, the progress line stuck on frame 416 of 664 while the log kept ticking (so
   it looked alive). Every piece is now tagged bt709 explicitly, and stage E is split into a
   video pass and an audio pass so there is no A/V interleave to stall on either.

Stages B/C are frame-exact: a dissolve of *nd* frames consumes the last *nd* frames of the
outgoing shot and the first *nd* of the incoming one, which is precisely what
`xfade(duration=d, offset=len−d)` does in the monolithic graph. Intermediates are crf 12, so the
only lossy step that matters is the final crf 18 encode.
